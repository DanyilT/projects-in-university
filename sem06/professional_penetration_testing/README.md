## What this module is for

Professional Penetration Testing is the third-year penetration testing module. It prepares for penetration testing and ethical hacking projects and is mapped to the syllabus of the CompTIA PenTest+ certification: planning and scoping, reconnaissance and footprinting, scanning and enumeration (Nmap), vulnerability scanning (Nessus and OpenVAS), exploitation with Metasploit, social engineering, password attacks, wireless attacks, attacks on the local host and privilege escalation, web and database attacks, and communication and reporting. The labs are done on a Kali Linux virtual machine in VirtualBox and on the TryHackMe platform. The lab sheets are markdown pages with an estimated time (the lecturer's lab pages), and every lab is submitted as a report with a cover page (lab, duration, module, instructor, date and submission date). The lectures were on Fridays (13:00 to 15:00), the labs on Mondays and Wednesdays in groups, and there was a drop-in tutorial each week. The week folders start at 0: week00 is the first teaching week (26 January 2026), and week09 is the week of 13 April, after the spring break. This folder holds the lab sheets, the lab reports (and the penetration test report of Lab 2b), the lab resources and the module resources (the Nmap, Nessus and OpenVAS guides and the NIST guide to security testing). The main assessment is a large practical test in exam week, a simulated penetration test on a small company network, wired and wireless: the module is 10% weekly lecture MCQ and engagement, 10% labs and 80% exam (20% theory, 60% practical). The end-of-semester exam sittings were on 18 and 19 May 2026 in four groups (9:00 to 13:00 and 14:00 to 18:00), each student with a static IP address on the exam network. A failed module can be repeated in the summer, with a 30% MCQ exam and a 70% practical pentest exam.

## Module overview

The learning outcomes of the module overview: describe each of the steps in the penetration testing methodology; recognise security vulnerabilities (weak configurations, unpatched systems) and the limitations of penetration testing techniques; develop tailored scoping and rules of engagement; conduct detailed reconnaissance with document metadata, search engines and other public sources; use a scanning tool such as Nmap for network sweeps, port scans, OS fingerprinting and version scanning; configure and launch a vulnerability scanner such as Nessus safely; explore target systems for information that furthers the test, establishes pivots and shows business risk; conduct password attacks (automated guessing, traditional and rainbow table cracking, pass-the-hash); and use wireless attack tools to discover access points and clients, crack WEP, WPA and WPA2 keys and exploit client machines in scope.

The expectations: a quick pace, about 6 to 8 hours of work a week on top of the lectures and labs, and a mostly practical module. The required textbook is CompTIA PenTest+ (PT0-002), second edition, by Ray Nutting (McGraw Hill, ISBN 978-1-264-27489-5). The PenTest+ exam (version PT0-003) is 165 minutes with 85 multiple choice questions and a pass mark of 750 out of 900.
